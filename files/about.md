# Colophon

This website presents a digital, visual corpus of approximately 470 medieval aldermen's deeds (*schepenakten*) from the city of Antwerp, dating from ca. 1300 to ca. 1355. These single-page parchment documents recorded transactions and agreements ratified by the city's aldermen and were written by a small group of professional scribes employed by the aldermen's bench — among whom, in all likelihood, the influential vernacular author [Jan van Boendale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_van_Boendale) (ca. 1275–ca. 1351). Boendale himself tells us that he worked "for many years" writing the aldermen's "letters" or deeds, and his involvement in Antwerp's administration is corroborated by municipal accounts and other records. The corpus was compiled in the context of a computational paleography study that seeks to identify Boendale's own hand among the surviving deeds, combining computer vision models with traditional philological analysis.

## Project context

The edition is embedded in two publicly funded research projects:

- *De vele gezichten van Boendale: het modelleren van de historische positionaliteit in Jan van Boendales (ca. 1280–1351?) oeuvre* (FWO doctoral fellowship 1133925N, [Caroline Van Dyck](https://www.uantwerpen.be/en/staff/caroline-vandyck_25944/), advised by Mike Kestemont and Remco Sleiderink). This project studies the historical positionality of Jan van Boendale's oeuvre using Digital Humanities methods.
- *Oneindige verhalen: de ontsluiting van ingewikkelde tekstoverleveringen en historiografische netwerken. Showcase: De totstandkoming en verspreiding van Jan van Boendales 'Brabantsche yeesten'* (FWO, 2025–2028, promotor: [Remco Sleiderink](https://www.uantwerpen.be/en/staff/remco-sleiderink/research/)). This project investigates the production, transmission, and instrumentalisation of medieval historical narratives using a combination of traditional philology and digital methods.


## Transcriptions and technology

The transcriptions presented on this website are documentary, strictly diplomatic: abbreviation glyphs are recorded without resolution, allographs are preserved, and the transcription standard is hyper-diplomatic in nature. These transcriptions were produced with the support of Handwritten Text Recognition (HTR) via [Transkribus](https://www.transkribus.org/). As with any automated transcription of historical handwriting, the results are not perfect and may contain recognition errors. Users are encouraged to consult the accompanying manuscript images for verification.

The web interface is powered by [Necturus](https://github.com/NoonShin/Necturus-Viewer), a free and open-source viewer for digital scholarly editions developed by [Nooshin Shahidzadeh Asadi](https://www.uantwerpen.be/en/staff/nooshin-shahidzadeh-asadi_23445/) (University of Antwerp). Necturus provides an interactive, line-by-line connection between manuscript images and their transcriptions.

## Image credits

The corpus was bootstrapped from a pre-existing image collection curated by former city archivist Jos Van den Nieuwenhuizen (1928–2026) and archivist Michel Oosterbosch. We also gratefully acknowledge significant image donations from Chris De Wulf and Kamiel Temmerman, as well as material from the public image archives of Antwerp heritage institutions. Given the diverse origins and varying quality of the photographic material (including black-and-white photographs, low-resolution scans, and scans of printed reproductions), the corpus is provided as-is as a useful but imperfect resource. These historic documents are in the public domain.
